# Confirmed Opt-In (Double Opt-In) — Design Spec

Date: 2026-08-12
Status: Draft, critical-reviewed (BLOCK → 9 fixes applied + 1 fresh gap found and closed during fix-up), pending re-verification
Trigger: SES/Comcast abuse complaint 2026-08-11 on the 3–10 Aug digest

---

## 1. Why

On 2026-08-11 a Comcast recipient marked the TradieIntel digest as spam. Amazon SES relayed the feedback-loop complaint and auto-suppressed the address.

Investigation of the live NitroSend list (brand 466, "GTH Digital" / TradieIntel) found seven contacts total:

| Email | Created | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| dwaite78@comcast.net | 2026-07-26 19:02Z | complained, SES-suppressed |
| abarringer17@icloud.com | 2026-07-26 05:30Z | US-pattern, never engaged |
| sez.eyo.c1.58@gmail.com | 2026-07-20 23:50Z | randomised address pattern |
| ghardiman7@gmail.com | 2026-06-21 | owner |
| rest-probe@example.com | 2026-06-20 | build probe |
| nitro-probe-sub@example.com | 2026-06-16 | build probe |
| nitro-probe-unsub@example.com | 2026-06-16 | build probe |

Genuine subscribers: zero. Two of the three non-owner signups were created hours apart on 26 July, both US addresses, on a newsletter about Australian trades. The pattern is automated form submission or third-party list-bombing, not human intent.

The 24h deliverability window recorded 4 sends and 1 complaint, a 25% complaint rate against an SES review threshold of 0.1%. Volume is too low for AWS to act, but `tradieintel.com.au` currently routes through NitroSend's **shared** sending pool rather than the account's own Resend infrastructure (`byo_routing.mismatch: true`), so the reputational damage lands on a shared resource and NitroSend's abuse handling is the near-term risk, not AWS.

Three distinct problems follow (the third added on review — see below):

1. **Deliverability.** Unconsented addresses generate complaints at a rate that threatens sending access.
2. **Compliance — verifiability.** [`subscribe.ts`](../../../src/pages/api/subscribe.ts) records `consent: true` and a timestamp, but that record is generated by whoever submits the form. A bot ticking a checkbox produces a consent record indistinguishable from a real one. Under the Spam Act 2003 (Cth) the sender must be able to demonstrate consent; an unverifiable self-asserted flag does not do that.
3. **Compliance — scope.** The live consent checkbox (`src/config/site.ts:15`) bundles two different senders into one tick: *"I agree to receive emails from Tradie Intel and GrokoryAI."* The flywheel strategy is explicit that this is not one consent event: **"A feeder subscription is consent to the feeder, not to GrokoryAI marketing"** (`processes/grokoryai/grokoryai-flywheel-strategy.md`, "Consent on every list hop"). Confirming a bundled checkbox would produce a *verified* record of a *non-compliant* consent — the build would fix problem 2 while leaving problem 3 baked in.

Confirmed opt-in solves 1 and 2: an address only reaches a send list after someone with access to that mailbox clicks a link, which is evidence a bot cannot manufacture. Splitting the checkbox (§3.3) solves 3.

The list has already been purged (2026-08-12): all six non-owner contacts are unsubscribed or suppressed, verified by querying `subscribed_email: true` and getting a single row.

## 2. Current state

`POST /api/subscribe` ([src/pages/api/subscribe.ts](../../../src/pages/api/subscribe.ts)):

1. Rejects non-POST
2. Discards submissions with a filled `website` honeypot field, returning a fake 200
3. Requires non-empty `email` and `consent === true`
4. Builds `SubscribeMeta` (consent flag, ISO timestamp, source, referrer, three UTM fields)
5. Calls `provider.subscribe(email, meta)` — the address is live and mailable immediately

The provider abstraction ([src/lib/email.ts](../../../src/lib/email.ts)) has five implementations behind `getProvider()`, selected by `EMAIL_PROVIDER`: `kit`, `mailchimp`, `memory`, `nitrosend`, `dual` (Kit primary + NitroSend secondary, fire-and-forget).

Confirmation behaviour is currently inconsistent across those implementations. `MailchimpProvider` already posts `status: 'pending'`, which is Mailchimp's native double opt-in. `KitProvider` and `NitrosendProvider` subscribe directly with no confirmation step. Whether a signup is confirmed therefore depends on an env var, which is not a defensible compliance position.

The only bot defence is the CSS honeypot. It did not stop the 26 July signups.

`src/lib/digest.ts:47-77` already implements a signed-token pattern for a different flow (digest approval links): `signApproveToken()` / `verifyApproveToken()`, HMAC-SHA256, base64url payload, `timingSafeEqual` comparison, expiry carried as an `exp` field inside the payload. §3.2 reuses this rather than inventing a second format.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Principle

Confirmation is owned by the application, not delegated to the provider.

The alternative — enabling each provider's native double opt-in — was rejected. It would produce three different behaviours across the three live providers (Mailchimp pending, a Kit form setting, NitroSend unverified), double-confirm under `dual`, and leave the consent evidence inside a vendor account that a future migration would lose. One application-level gate gives identical behaviour on every provider and keeps the evidence in systems we control.

### 3.2 Stateless confirmation tokens

No pending-subscribers table.

Generalise the existing helpers in `src/lib/digest.ts` into a shared `src/lib/token.ts` (`signToken(payload, secret)` / `verifyToken(token, secret)`), keeping the proven wire format and conventions:

- Wire format: `<payloadB64>.<sigB64url>` (single string, not split query params)
- Payload: JSON, base64url-encoded, carries its own `exp` (unix seconds) — do not derive expiry by subtracting an issued-at field; reuse the existing `exp`-in-payload convention
- Signature: HMAC-SHA256 over the payload string, base64url-encoded (not hex — match the existing helper)
- Verification: recompute HMAC, compare with `crypto.timingSafeEqual`, reject on length mismatch before comparing, then check `payload.exp` against current time

Confirmation-specific payload:

```ts
interface ConfirmTokenPayload {
  email: string;
  scope: 'tradieintel_digest' | 'grokoryai_commercial'; // which consent this token confirms
  source: string;
  referrer: string | null;
  utm_source: string | null;
  utm_medium: string | null;
  utm_campaign: string | null;
  iat: number; // signup request time — the source for subscriber_consents.requested_at (§3.6)
  exp: number; // iat + 48h
}
```

Carrying `scope` lets one signup produce up to two independent confirmation tokens (§3.3) instead of one bundled one. Carrying the four attribution fields (previously dropped) preserves Flow 1 traceability end to end — they are signed, so size is not a real constraint at this scale, and they survive into `subscriber_consents` (§3.6) rather than vanishing at the token boundary.

`digest.ts`'s existing `ApproveTokenPayload` type and `signApproveToken`/`verifyApproveToken` become thin wrappers over the shared `token.ts`, or are left as-is and `token.ts` is extracted as the common core — either is fine; the point is one HMAC implementation, not two.

The confirmation link:

```
{PUBLIC_SITE_URL}/confirm?t=<token>
```

This removes the pending table, the orphan-row cleanup job, and the expiry sweep. Expiry is a field comparison. An unconfirmed signup leaves no state anywhere, which is the correct outcome: a bot submission should cost us a single email and nothing else.

The token is a bearer credential for one address and one scope. It cannot be replayed against another address or reinterpreted as consent for the other scope, since both are inside the signed payload.

**`SUBSCRIBE_TOKEN_SECRET` must be a distinct value from `CRON_SECRET`** (which signs digest-approval tokens). Reusing it means one leaked secret forges both subscriber confirmations and digest approvals. Rotation trigger: post-incident (suspected leak) or on a security review; there is no scheduled cadence at this volume. Rotating invalidates every confirmation link currently in flight — acceptable (worst case, the subscriber re-submits the form), but note it as a known consequence, not a surprise.

Set `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` on `/confirm` — the token is in the URL, and without this header it leaks into the `Referer` of any outbound request the page makes (analytics, external links, etc).

### 3.2b Per-scope provider routing

**Gap found on review:** every `EmailProvider` implementation (`src/lib/email.ts`) bakes one list ID into its constructor, and `getProvider()` returns a single app-wide instance from one `EMAIL_LIST_ID`/`NITROSEND_LIST_ID` pair. There is currently no way to route `tradieintel_digest` and `grokoryai_commercial` confirmations to two different lists. §3.3 step 4 depends on this existing — it does not, so it needs to be built.

Minimal fix, keeping today's providers otherwise untouched:

- Change `getProvider()` to `getProvider(scope: ConsentScope): EmailProvider`, reading scope-specific env vars:
  - `tradieintel_digest` → existing `NITROSEND_LIST_ID` / `EMAIL_LIST_ID` (unchanged — this is the list that exists today and everything currently points at it)
  - `grokoryai_commercial` → new `NITROSEND_LIST_ID_COMMERCIAL` / `EMAIL_LIST_ID_COMMERCIAL`, both optional
- If the scope's list env var is unset, `getProvider()` returns `null` rather than throwing. `/confirm` treats a `null` provider as "audit the consent, skip the subscribe call" (see §3.3 step 4) — this lets the commercial checkbox ship and legally-valid consent get recorded now, even before a GrokoryAI commercial list is provisioned in NitroSend/Kit to actually receive it.
- `DualProvider` and `MailchimpProvider` take the same treatment: the scope determines which configured list ID (if any) each wraps.

This is additive to `email.ts`, not a rewrite of the existing providers' internals — only `getProvider()`'s signature and env-var lookup change.

### 3.3 Flow

**Signup form** — consent is split into two checkboxes, not one:

- **Required**: "Send me the TradieIntel weekly digest." (scope `tradieintel_digest`)
- **Optional**: "Also send me occasional emails from GrokoryAI about AI tools for trades businesses." (scope `grokoryai_commercial`)

This replaces the single bundled checkbox in `src/config/site.ts:15`. Only the digest subscription is required to submit the form; the commercial checkbox is opt-in to opt-in.

**Signup** — `POST /api/subscribe`

1. Existing checks unchanged: method, JSON parse, honeypot, email present
2. Require `consent_digest === true` (renamed from `consent`); `400` if missing — this is the digest scope and stays mandatory
3. Read `consent_commercial` (optional boolean, default `false`)
4. Add format validation via the existing `isValidEmail()` before any outbound call
5. Rate-limit (§3.5) — **before** any email is sent
6. Mint a `tradieintel_digest` token (§3.2); if `consent_commercial` is true, also mint a separate `grokoryai_commercial` token
7. Send the confirmation email(s) (§3.4) — one email if only the digest was checked, listing both actions if both were checked (see §3.4 for the combined-email approach; do not send two separate emails for one form submission)
8. Return `200 {ok: true}` — **do not** call `provider.subscribe()`

The response is identical whether or not the address already exists, so the endpoint cannot be used to enumerate subscribers.

**Confirmation** — `GET /confirm`

1. Verify signature; on failure render an error page, HTTP 400
2. Check `exp`; on failure render an "expired, sign up again" page with a link back to the form, HTTP 400
3. **Write the consent audit row first** (§3.6), keyed by `(email, scope)` — a consent record with no subsequent subscription is harmless; the reverse (subscribed with no consent record) is the exact failure this spec exists to prevent, so ordering matters
4. Call `provider.subscribe(email, meta)` scoped to this `scope`'s destination list (§3.2b), with `meta.consent_timestamp` set to **confirmation** time, not request time
   - `scope: 'grokoryai_commercial'` with no destination list configured (§3.2b) is not an error — the audit row from step 3 already proves consent even before a sending list exists to act on it. Log and skip the provider call rather than fail the confirmation
   - **On any other failure** (network error, provider 5xx, etc.): render a "something went wrong, try the link again" page, HTTP 502. Do **not** consume or invalidate the token — a retry of the same link must be able to complete step 4 again. The audit row from step 3 already exists and is untouched by a retry (upsert on `(email, scope)`, not insert-only)
5. Render a success page naming which scope(s) were confirmed

Confirmation is idempotent end to end: re-running steps 3–4 for the same `(email, scope)` on a retried or re-clicked link is safe. Step 3 upserts; step 4 re-subscribes an already-subscribed address, which every provider tolerates (`NitrosendProvider` treats 422-already-exists as success today; `KitProvider`'s upsert behaviour on duplicate is *unverified* — see Minor notes — confirm before relying on it, or wrap the call in a try/catch that treats a "already subscribed"-shaped error as success).

If two tokens were minted (both boxes checked), `/confirm` handles one scope per link/click — the confirmation email contains one link per scope (§3.4).

`GET` is used deliberately, since the link is clicked from an email client. The action is idempotent and non-destructive (per the retry-safety above), so the usual objection to state-changing GETs does not apply. Note that some corporate mail scanners pre-fetch links, which would auto-confirm; this is a known and accepted limitation of every link-based confirmation flow, and it is still strictly better than no confirmation.

### 3.4 Confirmation email

Sent via **Resend directly**, not NitroSend.

Rationale: this is transactional, not marketing. Keeping it on a separate path preserves the transactional/marketing separation that mailbox providers reward, avoids routing a one-off send through NitroSend's campaign machinery, and means confirmation delivery is unaffected if the marketing pool is ever throttled.

Requires `RESEND_API_KEY`. **This is not currently present** in `.env`, `.env.local`, or `.env.example` — a `ResendProvider` existed per the 2026-05-27 handover (`docs/superpowers/handovers/2026-05-27-resend-swap-handover.md`) but is no longer in `src/lib/email.ts`, and NitroSend's own status shows a Resend provider configured on its side, not necessarily a key usable directly from this app. **Source or re-provision the key before step 4 of §6** — do not assume it's already available.

From `hello@tradieintel.com.au`, matching the existing sender identity.

Content: what they signed up for, what arrives and how often, per confirmed action:
- If only `tradieintel_digest` was checked: one confirmation button.
- If both were checked: two clearly separated confirmation buttons/links, each independently labelled ("Confirm the weekly digest" / "Confirm GrokoryAI emails"), each carrying its own scoped token. The recipient can confirm one without the other.

Plus, in both cases: the raw URL(s) as fallback, and a line stating that no further email will be sent if they ignore it. That last line matters — it is what turns an unsolicited-looking confirmation into a defensible one for someone who never signed up.

Include `List-Unsubscribe` headers even on this message.

### 3.5 Rate limiting

Confirmed opt-in makes bot signups harmless to the list, but not free: a bot submitting a thousand addresses would trigger a thousand confirmation emails to people who never asked, which is itself a complaint source and the mechanism behind list-bombing attacks. **This must be live before `subscribe.ts` starts sending confirmation emails** — see resequenced §6.

Minimum viable control, in order:

1. **Per-address**: refuse to send a second confirmation to the same address within 15 minutes
2. **Per-IP**: cap at 5 signups per hour

Both need shared state across serverless invocations, so this is one small Supabase table (`subscribe_attempts`: email hash, IP hash, timestamp) with rows older than an hour swept on write. Hash both values rather than storing them raw, so the abuse-control table does not itself become a personal-information store.

**Fail-open on Supabase unavailability.** If the rate-limit check itself errors (DB unreachable), allow the signup through rather than blocking legitimate users on an infra blip — the honeypot and confirmed opt-in remain as backstops. Log the failure so a sustained outage is visible.

### 3.6 Consent audit record

New Supabase table `subscriber_consents`, one row per **confirmed (email, scope) pair**, upserted (not inserted) so retries and re-confirmations don't duplicate:

| Column | Notes |
|---|---|
| `email` | lowercased |
| `scope` | `tradieintel_digest` \| `grokoryai_commercial` — which sender this consent covers |
| `confirmed_at` | timestamptz, the legally significant timestamp; updated on re-confirmation |
| `requested_at` | from the token payload's `iat` — do **not** derive it as `exp - 48h`; the lifetime is tunable (§8 Q1) and that derivation would silently drift |
| `source` | from the token payload |
| `referrer` | from the token payload |
| `utm_source` / `utm_medium` / `utm_campaign` | from the token payload |
| `ip_hash` | hash of the confirming request's IP |
| `user_agent` | of the confirming request |

Unique constraint on `(email, scope)`.

This is the Spam Act evidence trail and it survives a provider migration. The provider's own record does not. Because `scope` is a column, the digest and any future GrokoryAI commercial consent are independently provable — this is what closes problem 3 from §1, not just problem 2.

## 6. Sequencing

Rate limiting (previously step 6) is moved before the switch-over (previously step 5) — shipping a confirmation-email-sending endpoint with no throttle, even briefly, reopens the list-bombing-amplification risk described in §3.5.

1. `token.ts` + tests — pure, no dependencies
2. Migration `0004` (`subscriber_consents` with `scope`, `subscribe_attempts`)
3. `getProvider(scope)` change in `email.ts` (§3.2b) + tests — returns `null` on an unconfigured scope instead of throwing
4. Source/confirm `RESEND_API_KEY` availability; `confirmation-email.ts`, verified with a real send to a mailbox you control
5. `confirm.astro` + tests (including the audit-before-subscribe ordering test and the null-provider/skip-subscribe path for an unconfigured commercial list)
6. `rate-limit.ts` + tests
7. Rewire `subscribe.ts` (split checkboxes, token mint, confirmation send) + update/rewrite the three tests identified in §5
8. Front-end: two checkboxes in `EmailCapture.astro`, `site.ts` copy split, privacy page
9. End-to-end on preview: submit with both boxes checked → receive one email with two links → click digest link → verify subscribed in NitroSend + `subscriber_consents` row with `scope=tradieintel_digest` → click commercial link → verify second `subscriber_consents` row with `scope=grokoryai_commercial` (or, if `EMAIL_LIST_ID_COMMERCIAL` is left unset for launch, verify the audit row lands with no provider call and no error). Also test: kill the digest provider call (bad `NITROSEND_API_KEY`) and confirm the audit row still lands and the user sees the retry page, not a false success.

Steps 1–6 are additive and ship safely on their own. Step 7 is the switch-over and must not ship before step 6.

## 7. Out of scope

- **Cloudflare Turnstile** — still worth adding, but confirmed opt-in plus rate limiting addresses the immediate risk, and Turnstile is an independent change.
- **Moving `tradieintel.com.au` off NitroSend's shared pool onto the account's own Resend.** Separate decision, flagged in §1 and worth taking soon regardless of this work.
- **Re-confirming existing subscribers.** Moot — the list is one address, the owner's.
- **Backfilling `scope` for historical consent.** Not attempted — the historical record predates the split and its true scope is unrecoverable; this spec only fixes consent going forward.

## 8. Open questions

1. **48h token lifetime** — long enough for a weekend signup, short enough to bound replay. Confirm or adjust.
2. **`subscribe_attempts` sweep on write** — fine at current volume; revisit if signup volume ever becomes real.
3. **Existing `dual` provider mode** — spec assumes it stays. If Kit is being retired, that simplifies `getProvider()` independently of this work.
4. **`KitProvider` duplicate-subscribe behaviour is unverified** (§3.3) — confirm it upserts rather than erroring before relying on idempotent re-confirmation under `EMAIL_PROVIDER=kit` or `dual`.
5. **`token.ts` vs `digest.ts` refactor** — §4 leaves the choice open (extract shared core vs. two parallel implementations). Decide at implementation time; do not ship both unrefactored.
6. **No GrokoryAI commercial list exists yet in NitroSend/Kit.** §3.2b makes this non-blocking (audit-only until a list is provisioned) but it means the "optional" checkbox currently confirms consent with no live send capability behind it. Decide whether to provision the list before launch or ship consent-capture-only and provision later — either is consistent with the spec, but pick one before §6 step 9's E2E test.
